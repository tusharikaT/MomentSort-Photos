
# USER INTERVIEWS — MomentSort (Google Photos Retrieval)
## Qualitative | In-Person | 5 Interviews | Primary Research

---

## INTERVIEW GUIDE

8 questions. Around 25 minutes. Semi-structured, meaning don't read this like a script. Follow the person. Q2 is the whole interview. Everything else is context or cleanup.

**Q1.** How long have you been on Google Photos, and roughly how many photos do you think you have?

**Q2.** Tell me about the last time you tried to find an old photo and it got frustrating or took way longer than it should. Walk me through exactly what happened, step by step.

**Q3.** What did you type first? What came back when you searched?

**Q4.** At what point did you feel stuck? What did you do after that?
*(Keep asking "and then what?" until the full episode is out.)*

**Q5.** Close your eyes for a second. That photo you were looking for — describe it to me like I can't see it.

**Q6.** Was there anything you knew about it that you just couldn't figure out how to type?

**Q7.** Have you ever fully given up on finding a photo? What did you do instead?

**Q8.** If you could change one thing about how Google Photos search works, what would it be?

**+ Observation.** Can you show me on your phone right now? Search for any photo from more than a year ago that you haven't looked at in a while. Talk me through what you're thinking.
*(Watch silently. Note what they type first, how many times they retype, whether they scroll or refine, and when they stop.)*

## INT-01  Ravi

**Age:** 24 &emsp; **Occupation:** Software Developer &emsp; **City:** Bangalore

---

**Q  How long have you been on Google Photos? And how many photos do you have?**

**A**  Three, four years maybe. Photos? No idea. My phone backs everything up and I never clean it out, so it could easily be ten, eleven thousand. Could be more. I genuinely don't know and I don't want to know.

**Q  Tell me about the last time you tried to find an old photo and it got frustrating. Walk me through exactly what happened.**

**A**  Okay so this was literally last week. I was looking for a photo from a Coorg trip we did two years back. I remembered it clearly, the waterfall, the specific angle, the people. So I typed "waterfall Coorg" and it came back with... everything. Just a wall of photos. Some were from that trip, some were from god knows when.

**Q  What did you type first?**

**A**  "Waterfall Coorg." Thought that would be specific enough. It wasn't.

**Q  And what came back?**

**A**  Eighty, hundred photos maybe. They all looked the same. Greenery, water, people. I started scrolling. I scrolled for a while. Added the year. Tried different words. None of it helped.

**Q  At what point did you feel stuck?**

**A**  Immediately after the results loaded honestly. I looked at that grid and just thought, okay, this is going to take forever. And I was right.

**Q  What did you do after that?**

**A**  Gave up on search. Went to the timeline, found the rough month, started scrolling from there. That took another twenty minutes. Found it eventually. But like, why. Why does finding my own photo have to feel like this.

**Q  Close your eyes. That photo — describe it to me like I can't see it.**

**A**  The three of us near the waterfall. There's mist everywhere. We're wet from the spray. It was afternoon, bright. My friend was wearing a yellow raincoat. Very specific photo. I can see it in my head right now.

**Q  Was there anything you knew but couldn't figure out how to type?**

**A**  The mist. The "wet from spray" thing. How do you type that? I tried "mist" actually, didn't do anything useful. You just can't type a visual feel into a search bar. That's the whole problem.

**Q  Have you ever fully given up on finding a photo?**

**A**  Yeah, plenty of times. I either text whoever was in the photo and ask them to send it, or I just accept it's gone and move on. I'm not spending forty minutes every time. That's not a thing.

**Q  If you could change one thing about how search works?**

**A**  Give me something to narrow down after I search. Not before, after. Like, I get a hundred photos back, let me say "only 2022" or "only outdoors" or "only this group of people." Right now I get the results and I'm just alone with this massive grid. No next step. That's where it completely breaks down.

---

**OBSERVATION — INT-01**

Ravi opens Google Photos and types "Coorg trip" immediately, no hesitation. Gets back a big grid. He scrolls down once slowly, then just shakes his head. "See this? This is what I mean. What am I supposed to do with this." He adds "2023" to the query. Still a lot. He taps three photos one by one to check the dates, sighs, closes the app. "At this point I'd just text my friends. That's genuinely faster."

---

**ANALYSIS NOTES**

- Big library, specific memory, very clear about where it breaks ("alone with the massive grid" is a slide-ready quote)
- Workaround is instant: text people or accept loss. No emotional spiraling, just practical frustration.
- His ideal feature is basically MomentSort chips, described from scratch without any prompting.
- Strongly aligned. Would engage with chips immediately if they existed.

## INT-02  Amulya

**Age:** 28 &emsp; **Occupation:** Schoolteacher &emsp; **City:** Hyderabad

---

**Q  How long have you been using Google Photos?**

**A**  Since 2017 I think, maybe 2018. It's like that thing where you add a little every day and suddenly the bag is full, you know? I'd guess eight or nine thousand photos. Mostly school events, family things. It adds up without you even realising.

**Q  Tell me about the last time searching for an old photo got frustrating.**

**A**  So we had Bonalu at school this year and I wanted to find a photo from two years back. Me and my colleague Pavithra both wore matching traditional outfits by complete coincidence. It was this funny moment, everyone loved it. I wanted to share it as a throwback this Bonalu. So I opened Google Photos and searched "Bonalu school" and just... it was a lot.

**Q  What did you type first, and what came back?**

**A**  "Bonalu school." And it came back with everything. My family Bonalu photos mixed in with the school ones. I couldn't tell which was which from the thumbnails. They all just look like festival photos, right? Women in sarees, decorations, all of it blending together.

**Q  When did you feel stuck?**

**A**  When I realised I had to open each photo individually just to check the date. That's when I knew this would take a while. It's a bit like, you know when you're looking for one specific book and all the covers look the same? That's what it felt like.

**Q  What did you do next?**

**A**  I added "2023" to the search. That helped a little, the count came down. But school and family photos were still mixed. I gave up on search eventually and just went to the timeline, scrolled back to around September 2023, went from there. Took me close to twenty minutes total.

**Q  Close your eyes. Describe that photo.**

**A**  Me and Pavithra both in white and red traditional dress, standing in the school courtyard. Big marigold decoration behind us. We're both laughing because someone had just pointed out we matched. It was a sunny afternoon. Very warm light.

**Q  Was there anything you knew but couldn't put into words to type?**

**A**  "School courtyard Bonalu matching outfits 2023." I mean I could type all of that but it won't work, right? The app doesn't understand what I'm actually describing visually. I can see the photo so clearly in my head. But none of those words pull it up.

**Q  Have you ever fully given up on a search?**

**A**  Yes. There was a photo from our school's anniversary event, 2019. I searched for over an hour. Finally I just asked three colleagues one by one. One of them had it. I gave up on finding my own copy. That felt a bit strange actually, having to ask someone else for a photo I took on my own phone.

**Q  If you could change one thing?**

**A**  After I search and get back a hundred photos, give me a way to say "only school ones" or "only this year." Right now the app just dumps everything on me and walks away, you know? There's nothing to help me from there. I'm on my own.

---

**OBSERVATION — INT-02**

Amulya types "school event Hyderabad" into the search bar, takes a moment thinking about what words to use. A large grid loads. She starts scrolling slowly, tilting her phone slightly. "How am I supposed to tell which year this is from just by looking at it?" She taps a few photos to check dates. Tries "school function 2023." Still many. "I know it's somewhere in here. I just can't reach the exact one." She doesn't find the target in the observation window but stays calm about it.

---

**ANALYSIS NOTES**

- School and personal photos mixing is a real, recurring contamination problem for her segment.
- Workaround: asked colleagues, WhatsApp groups. Social retrieval fallback.
- "The app dumps everything and walks away" is a direct quote worth keeping for the slide.
- Strongly aligned. Would benefit immediately from year and context chips above the flood.

## INT-03  Siddhant

**Age:** 25 &emsp; **Occupation:** MBA Student, second year &emsp; **City:** Hyderabad

---

**Q  How long have you been on Google Photos?**

**A**  Since 2020. Library is probably three, four thousand. I delete things I don't like. I'm not a hoarder. My library is cleaner than most people's honestly.

**Q  Tell me about a time searching for a photo got frustrating.**

**A**  So, okay. "Frustrating" is strong. It was more like... mildly insulting. We did this batch trip to Araku valley, first semester of MBA. I wanted a specific group photo from that trip. Searched "Araku," mostly got the right results. But also mixed in were some random photos from when I happened to pass through that area for something else. So the results were right in spirit. Just not clean.

**Q  What did you type, and what came back?**

**A**  "Araku." Then "Araku 2024." Second one helped, got it down to maybe twenty photos. But I still had to open eight of them individually to find the right group. Which, fine, I found it. But it shouldn't take eight taps to confirm something I already knew.

**Q  At what point did you feel stuck?**

**A**  Not stuck exactly. More like... I gave the app ninety percent of what it needed and it still made me do the last bit by hand. That's the problem. The distance between "close results" and "exact result" is entirely on me. The app just stops helping at that point.

**Q  And what did you do?**

**A**  Opened photos one by one. Checked faces, checked dates. Found it on the fifth try. Took maybe twelve minutes total. Not a disaster, but pointless effort.

**Q  Close your eyes. Describe the photo.**

**A**  Sixteen of us at this viewpoint. Valley in the background. Late afternoon, that golden light. Someone in the group had a bright orange jacket, which makes the photo distinctive. I remembered that detail clearly.

**Q  Was there anything you couldn't type?**

**A**  No. That's the thing. I had the year, the place, the approximate vibe. I had enough. The issue wasn't what I remembered. The issue was that once I gave the app all that, there was no next step. No "here are three groups of results, pick one." Just a flat pile and good luck.

**Q  Have you ever fully given up?**

**A**  Yeah, once or twice. But honestly if I can't find it in ten minutes I just accept it and move on. I'm not particularly attached to any specific photo.

**Q  If you could change one thing?**

**A**  Group the results by occasion. Not by date, not alphabetically. By trip. Like, "this batch of photos is clearly from one trip, this other batch is from a different time." I would have tapped that immediately. That's what I wanted.

---

**OBSERVATION — INT-03**

Siddhant types "college trip Hyderabad" very deliberately, like he's already thought about it. Gets about thirty photos back. Scrolls through. "See, these are from two different trips mixed together. Same location, different people." He taps photos to check dates, finds what he was looking for in about two minutes. "Found it. But look at what I had to do." He points at the grid. "I had to manually figure out which trip was which. That's the app's job, not mine."

---

**ANALYSIS NOTES**

- Same core problem, smaller library so the cost is lower. Partially aligned.
- Described MomentSort clusters without knowing it: "group by occasion, not by date."
- Low emotional investment but technically identical pain. Library growth will move him into the core segment.
- Worth using his quote about "the app stops helping" for the problem definition slide.

## INT-04  Vaishnavi

**Age:** 22 &emsp; **Occupation:** Content Creator &emsp; **City:** Bangalore

---

**Q  How long have you been on Google Photos?**

**A**  Okay so I use it but also I kind of don't, you know? Like it backs up automatically but I live on Instagram so. Um. I've never actually checked how many photos I have. A lot I think? I take a lot of behind-the-scenes stuff for content, screenshots, everything. Oh wait, do screenshots count? Yeah okay probably like five thousand then.

**Q  Tell me about a time searching for a photo got frustrating.**

**A**  Okay so it was a screenshot actually. It was a cab booking confirmation, I needed it for a reimbursement at college. And I had no idea when exactly I'd taken it. I knew it was around June or July but that's it. Not like I noted it down.

**Q  What did you type, what came back?**

**A**  "Receipt." Nothing useful. "Cab." Nothing. I tried "Ola" I think. No. Screenshots are just invisible to Google Photos search, I feel like. Like it doesn't read the text that's inside the screenshot, right?

**Q  When did you feel stuck?**

**A**  The moment "receipt" returned nothing. Immediately. So I just scrolled. Went back to June, looked through everything. Found it eventually but oh my god it took a while. And I kept stopping to look at old photos and going "aww" at them which did not help.

**Q  Describe the screenshot.**

**A**  It was the Ola app confirmation screen. Yellow background. The booking was confirmed, destination was airport. There was a price breakdown. Very specific. But how would I search for that? I can describe it perfectly and the search bar has no idea what I'm talking about.

**Q  Was there anything you couldn't put into words?**

**A**  Everything! Like I knew exactly what it looked like but none of those words actually work. "Yellow background cab screenshot airport." That's not going to find it. I know exactly what I'm looking for and the app just shrugs.

**Q  Have you ever fully given up?**

**A**  For screenshots, all the time. For actual photos of people and places, search is actually fine for me. But documents, handwritten notes, screenshots — I've given up so many times. I just scroll now. Or I check WhatsApp, someone usually has it.

**Q  If you could change one thing?**

**A**  Make it read the text inside screenshots. If I type "Ola airport" it should find the screenshot where those exact words appear. That would fix like seventy percent of my problems with it honestly.

---

**OBSERVATION — INT-04**

Vaishnavi opens Google Photos, goes straight to the search bar. Types "trip Bangalore." A bunch of photos load. "Oh these are cute!" She briefly taps one to look at it, then catches herself. "Wait okay, back to this." She scrolls through the results. "These are all from different trips though, see? This is Coorg, this is somewhere else." Tries "Bangalore trip 2024." Finds a photo she likes in about two minutes. "Okay I found one. But that wasn't actually what I was looking for. I just found something else that looked good."

---

**ANALYSIS NOTES**

- Divergent. Her pain is screenshots and documents not being indexed, not the result flood problem.
- Library is mid-size but her search behavior is low-intensity. She moves on fast.
- MomentSort doesn't address her main pain. She needs OCR or text-in-image search.
- Useful contrast case for the slide: shows the photo retrieval problem has multiple distinct sub-problems.
